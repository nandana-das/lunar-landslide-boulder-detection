Stage 2
Backbone Pre-training
Initial fine-tuning of YOLO26n, YOLOv8n, YOLOv5s and RT-DETR backbones on combined LROC lunar source data with frozen feature extractors (LR=1e-3, 50 epochs).
Frozen Feature Tuning
Stage 3 • Core Contribution
Multi-Reference Histogram Matching
20-tile averaged OHRC photometric reference profile. Specification applied across all training images to eliminate sensor, albedo, and phase-angle domain shifts.
Bridge Sensor Domain Gap
Stage 4
Domain-Adapted Fine-Tuning
Full end-to-end unfreezing with cosine annealing schedule (LR=1e-4, 50 epochs) on domain-matched lunar images. Benchmarked against held-out lunar test split.
All Layers Unfrozen

OHRC Target Inference

💡 Core Scientific Insight: CNN Inductive Bias vs. Vision Transformer Cross-Domain Stability

Benchmarking across both CNN (YOLO26n, YOLOv5s, YOLOv8n) and Vision Transformer (RT-DETR-L) architectures reveals a crucial physical finding for planetary remote sensing:

  • Domain Generalization Gap: On the domain-adapted lunar validation split, RT-DETR-L lags YOLO26n by -15.6% mAP@0.5 (0.4843 vs. 0.6400).
  • Regolith Texture Hallucination: On 31,769 unlabeled OHRC tiles, RT-DETR-L triggered 436,896 detections across 80.2% of all tiles (compared to 4,565 on YOLO26n). Without localized convolutional inductive priors, transformer global cross-attention layers mistake fine regolith albedo ripples and crater micro-shadows for candidate rockfall boulders.
  • Conclusion for Mission Deployment: Compact CNNs with localized receptive fields (specifically YOLO26n) are vastly superior for zero-target-annotation planetary remote sensing, delivering tightly calibrated false-positive rejection under cross-sensor transfer.

REGIONAL STUDY South Pole vs. Equatorial Detection Divergence
Photometric & topographic divergence across 12 OHRC products • results/confidence_analysis/

Geological & Photometric Divergence

South Pole (lat ≈ -70°S) • 6 Products Incidence: 78°–84°

Low solar elevation angles produce deep, elongated shadows. The high contrast between sunlit boulder rims and black shadow pockets provides unambiguous structural cues, yielding high mean confidence (0.288–0.314) and strong model consensus.

Equatorial (lat ≈ +60°N) • 6 Products Incidence: 59°–68°

Steeper solar illumination reduces shadow extent, shifting the visual signature toward subtle regolith textural variations. YOLOv8n becomes highly sensitive to crater ripples and small mounds (81% tile hit rate), whereas YOLO26n remains disciplined and calibrated.

Key Presentation Finding: Transfer learning without target domain labels is fundamentally governed by illumination geometry. Multi-Reference Histogram Matching successfully equalized the first-order sensor response, but polar low-angle shadow physics remains the most reliable regime for automated rockfall classification.
